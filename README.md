# Hackathon Challenge on AI Automation

## AI Job Market & Workforce Transition Analysis

### Project Overview

This project explores how artificial intelligence and automation may influence the modern job market. Using a dataset of 500 job records, we analysed AI adoption, automation risk, job-growth projections, salaries, industries, remote-friendliness and required skills.

The goal is to provide data-driven insights that can help workers, employers and training providers better understand changing workforce trends and the importance of skills development.

## Problem Statement

As AI adoption continues to transform the workplace, workers and organisations need reliable insights into how different jobs may be affected by automation, changing job-growth projections, salary patterns and evolving skill requirements. Without accessible data-driven information, workers may struggle to identify skills and career areas that can help them adapt to changes in the labour market.

## Proposed Solution

We propose an AI Job Market Insights Dashboard that uses data analysis and visualisation to explore AI adoption, automation risk, job-growth projections, salaries, industries and required skills.

The solution aims to make workforce insights easier to understand and support better-informed skills development and workforce planning.

## Key Findings

- Transportation had the highest proportion of jobs classified as high automation risk at 48.7%.
- Jobs with high AI adoption had the highest proportion of projected decline at 37.4%.
- Jobs with low AI adoption had the highest proportions of projected growth and stability, both at 35.1%.
- Low AI adoption jobs had the highest average salary at approximately $93,354.
- JavaScript had the highest average salary among the required-skill categories at approximately $98,944.
- Python and Project Management were among the most frequently required skills, each appearing in 60 jobs.
- The relationship between AI adoption and automation risk was not simply linear.

## Dataset

The analysis uses a dataset containing 500 job records and 10 variables covering job titles, industries, company size, location, AI adoption, automation risk, required skills, salary, remote-friendliness and job-growth projections.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows that the impact of AI across jobs is not uniform. Different industries and skill categories display different patterns in automation risk, salaries and projected job growth.

Understanding these patterns can help workers and organisations prepare for changes in the labour market through informed skills development and workforce planning.
